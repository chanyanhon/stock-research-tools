# Stock Research

A research tool for evaluating large public companies through company-specific financial metrics, valuation evidence, and documented investment reasoning.

## Research concepts

**Price-to-earnings ratio (P/E)**:
A share price divided by earnings per share. The earnings basis and period must be stated because reported and adjusted earnings, and past and forecast earnings, produce different ratios.

**Trailing P/E**:
A P/E ratio based on earnings reported for the preceding twelve months.

**Forward P/E**:
A P/E ratio based on forecast earnings for a stated future period.

**Adjusted EPS**:
Issuer-reported earnings per share adjusted to exclude specified items. The issuer's definition can vary across companies and periods.

**PEG ratio**:
A forward P/E ratio divided by expected annualized EPS growth over a stated forecast horizon. The growth horizon and estimate source are part of its meaning.

**Core metric**:
A financial measure that remains useful as a baseline for understanding a company's performance or valuation.

**Context metric**:
A financial or operating measure used to examine a current company or industry question, such as AI infrastructure spending and its effect on free cash flow.

**Metric set**:
The selected collection of core and context metrics used to analyze a company.

**Valuation percentile**:
The rank of a valuation measure within a stated reference distribution, such as the company's own historical values. It describes relative position, not by itself whether a stock is undervalued or overvalued.

**Peer group**:
A set of companies selected for meaningful comparison based on similarities in business model and relevant economic drivers.

**Investment rating**:
A company-level Buy, Hold, or Sell view over the agreed investment horizon, supported by business prospects, valuation evidence, and risks. It is not tailored to an individual investor's portfolio or circumstances.
